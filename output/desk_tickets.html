Reflection

My take after running the Campus Customs agent team on all three tickets, over a few rounds of fixing and re-running.

1. How would you evaluate the performance of the agents on each ticket, and why?

Overall the agents got better every time I ran the tickets again. The first few runs had a lot of mistakes, and most of them came from the agents not looking at the other tickets or skipping an agent they were supposed to use.

  • #101 Bulldog tee: started bad, ended good. In the early runs the team bought a white tee for $8 and took the money out of the account, even though the ticket never asked for it. Invoice 501 was already the reprint of that exact tee, so it was a duplicate order. Another time the Boss skipped Inventory completely and went straight to Accounting, so nobody checked the stock. By the last run the team checked the stock, paid invoice 501 as the tee order (with my approval) and told Tauhid it would be ready about 5 days after the restock is released.
  • #102 Rent: the best one. This one worked pretty much every time. The Boss sent it to Facilities, Facilities checked the lease ($2,400 due in 2 days), Accounting prepared the payment and I approved it. It's simple, with one lease, one payment and no customer, so there was less for the agents to get wrong.
  • #103 Hoodies: good decisions, sloppy at the end. This was the hardest ticket because it depends on the other two. In the early runs the agents didn't connect the blocked hoodie supplier to #101's unpaid bill, and one run only ordered 6 hoodies because of the extra $8 tee. In the final run the thinking was right: it tried all 12, saw only $160 was left, ordered 7, and gave the club a 10% goodwill discount on the last 5. But Accounting typed the wrong vendor id, so the $154 went to Elm City Gifts (the mug supplier) instead of Bulldog Print Co.

2. For each ticket, how did Actual compare to the Expected plan you wrote earlier?

  • #101: In my first iteration Actual didn't match Expected, because the agents debited an extra tee shirt from the account. That got fixed in later iterations. In the final run it matched: Inventory found 0 in size S, Accounting prepared the $840 for invoice 501, Customer Service drafted the message, I approved both and the ticket resolved. One small difference: the message to Tauhid sounds a bit clunky ("provided we can release the restock today"), but it does say about 5 days and September 5 like I expected.
  • #102: This matched Expected exactly. Facilities checked the lease, Accounting prepared the $2,400, I approved it, the rent due date moved to 2026-10-02, and Inventory and Customer Service stayed out of it like I planned.
  • #103: The numbers matched my plan: 8 now + 7 restocked = 15 of 20, $154 for the 7 hoodies, a goodwill discount, and cash ending at $6. What didn't match was the supplier. I expected the order to go to Bulldog Print Co (5 days, September 5), but it went to Elm City Gifts, whose lead time is 3 days. So the club was told September 5 while the order the system actually placed says September 3. An earlier run also only ordered 6 hoodies instead of 7 because of the extra tee.

3. What would have been simpler as one agent with tools, and why?

With one agent there's no delegating, so the Boss wouldn't need an extra step to figure out who should do what. Having agents talk to each other while several tasks are going on makes the whole process more complicated. There are real communication issues: each agent can read what another agent said in its own way, and some agents just get ignored. That's exactly what caused some of my wrong results, like Inventory being skipped on #101 or Accounting passing along the wrong vendor id on #103. One agent holding all the facts itself wouldn't have these hand-off problems. It would also use far fewer tokens, because right now every agent re-explains the same facts to the next one.

4. Three new problems Campus Customs might face that this agent team could solve with the tools I built

  • A big order comes in right before rent is due. If rent is due before any money comes in from customers, and an order to the supplier has to be placed, the team won't place that order until the rent money is set aside. Accounting uses get_lease and get_cash_balance, and the board itself refuses a purchase order that would eat into the rent.
  • A customer is unhappy about waiting and wants something for it. The team can decide how much of a discount to give out of goodwill. Accounting prices it from the real cost with get_pricing, the board refuses anything under the cost floor, and it only goes to the customer after I approve it.
  • Someone orders a Crest Mug, which is at 0 in stock. This is a different item and a different supplier, and the same tools handle it. Inventory finds 0 on hand with check_stock, finds Elm City Gifts with list_vendors, and checks they can ship in 3 days with check_vendor_can_ship. Accounting prices the restock at $3.50 a mug for my approval, and Customer Service tells the customer it will be ready about 3 days after we order.

5. Three new problems this agent team could not solve with the tools I built, and the tools and agents I'd need

  • Recommending something else when an item is out of stock. If a customer wants a mug and we have none, the team can't suggest something similar they could buy instead. I'd add a find_similar_products(sku, max_price) tool that searches the inventory and pricing tables for items in stock at a similar price, and let the Inventory agent use it so Customer Service can show those options to the customer. The database would also need a category or description for each product, because right now it only has names.
  • The supplier delivering late. Campus Customs might get a delayed order from the supplier, and the agents can't predict that or fix logistics problems. Right now they just trust lead_days. I'd add a deliveries table (when each order was placed and when it actually arrived) and a get_delivery_history(vendor_id) tool. Then the Accounting agent, or a new Logistics agent, could see whether a supplier has been late with deliveries or payments before and add that delay to the date we give the customer.
  • Hearing back from the customer. The team asks the Yale AI Club whether they want the hoodies in stages or all at once, but there's no way for the club's answer to come back into the system, so the ticket just closes. I'd add a customer replies table with log_customer_reply(ticket_id, reply) and reopen_ticket(ticket_id) tools. Customer Service would record what the customer said, and the Boss would reopen the ticket and act on their choice, for example ordering the last 5 hoodies once there's cash.
